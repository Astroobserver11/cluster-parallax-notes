# 10 Best Candidates for High-Cadence Optical Pulsar Photometry on the Apache Point 3.5 m Telescope

**Date:** 2026-10-25

Ranked by optical brightness, confirmed or strongly predicted pulsed emission, and accessibility from APO (latitude ~32.8°N). Magnitudes are approximate V/B; periods given for cadence planning.

## Ranked List

1. **Crab (PSR B0531+21 / J0534+2200)** — V ≈ 16.5, P ≈ 33 ms  
   Brightest and highest-SNR target. Ideal for pulse-profile, harmonics, phase-lag, and multi-filter work.

2. **PSR J1023+0038** (transitional millisecond pulsar) — V ≈ 17–22 (state-dependent), P ≈ 1.69 ms  
   Confirmed optical pulsations. Excellent for studying accretion-disk vs. magnetospheric emission across state changes.

3. **Vela (PSR B0833-45)** — V ≈ 23.6, P ≈ 89 ms  
   Classic optical pulsar with well-measured double-peaked profile. Southern; limited elevation and observing windows from APO.

4. **PSR B0540-69** (LMC) — V ≈ 22–23, P ≈ 50 ms  
   Extragalactic optical pulsar. Southern and distant; feasible but demanding.

5. **Geminga (PSR J0633+1746)** — V ≈ 25.5, P ≈ 237 ms  
   Northern, radio-quiet. Confirmed optical pulsations; good for multi-band or polarization studies under excellent conditions.

6. **PSR B0656+14** — V ≈ 25, P ≈ 385 ms  
   Northern middle-aged pulsar with detected optical pulses. Comparable difficulty to Geminga; useful for SED and pulse-shape comparisons.

7. **PSR B1055-52** — V ≈ 24.9, P ≈ 197 ms  
   Optical/UV detection with reported pulsed emission. Moderately southern.

8. **PSR B1509-58** — V ≈ 25.7, P ≈ 151 ms  
   Faint optical counterpart with reported pulsed emission. Challenging but within reach for a deep program under good conditions.

9. **PSR B1929+10** — V ≈ 25.6, P ≈ 227 ms  
   Northern and relatively nearby. Optical identification exists; pulsed fraction less secure—suitable for confirmation or upper-limit work.

10. **PSR J0437-4715** — V ≈ 21, P ≈ 5.8 ms  
    Brightest known millisecond-pulsar optical counterpart. Far southern; only marginally accessible from APO (very low elevation).

## Planning Notes for APO 3.5 m
- Highest-priority northern/equatorial targets: Crab, J1023+0038, Geminga, B0656+14.
- Vela and B0540-69 are scientifically strong but elevation-limited.
- Periods ≲ 50 ms require high-speed photon-counting or fast-frame cameras.
- Precise radio/gamma-ray ephemerides and careful background control (nebula for Crab, sky for fainter objects) are essential for folding.

These ten cover the known optical pulsars realistically addressable by a 3.5 m telescope in the high-cadence pulsed-counterpart regime.
